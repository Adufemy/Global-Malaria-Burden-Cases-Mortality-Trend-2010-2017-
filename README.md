# Global-Malaria-Burden-Cases-Mortality-Trend-2010-2017-
An interactive malaria analytics dashboard examining global malaria cases, estimated deaths, regional burden, and country-level trends from 2010–2017. The analysis covers 107 countries and highlights Africa as the highest-burden region, supporting data-driven public health insights.
🦟 Global Malaria Burden: Cases, Mortality & Trends (2010–2017)
Project Overview

The Global Malaria Burden Dashboard is a public health data analytics project designed to examine the global burden of malaria through reported malaria cases, estimated deaths, geographical distribution, and yearly trends.

The dashboard analyzes malaria data covering 107 countries from 2010 to 2017 and provides an interactive view of how malaria cases and estimated deaths changed over time and across WHO regions.

The analysis highlights Africa as the region with the highest malaria burden and identifies countries with the largest reported malaria case volumes.

Project Objective

The primary objective of this project is to transform malaria statistics into an interactive and easy-to-understand dashboard that can help stakeholders:

Understand the global malaria burden.
Monitor malaria cases over time.
Examine estimated malaria deaths over time.
Compare malaria burden across WHO regions.
Identify countries with the highest number of malaria cases.
Identify regions with the highest estimated malaria mortality.
Support evidence-based public health planning.
Highlight areas requiring stronger malaria prevention and intervention strategies.

Story of the Data

The dataset represents malaria statistics across countries and years, allowing the analysis to examine how malaria cases and estimated deaths vary geographically and over time.

The dashboard covers the period 2010–2017 and includes 107 countries. The information is aggregated by year, country, and WHO region, making it possible to compare malaria burden across different geographical areas.

The dashboard tells a clear story: malaria remains heavily concentrated in particular regions, with Africa identified as the highest-burden WHO region in the analysis.

Potential Analytical Questions
Question 1

How have malaria cases changed between 2010 and 2017?

Potential Insight

The malaria cases trend shows an overall decline from the beginning of the period, reaching a lower point around the middle of the period before increasing again toward 2017.

This suggests that malaria case reduction was not completely consistent throughout the study period.

Question 2

How have estimated malaria deaths changed between 2010 and 2017?

Potential Insight

Estimated malaria deaths show a general downward trend throughout the period.

This suggests progress in reducing malaria-related mortality despite the continued high number of malaria cases.

Question 3

Which WHO region has the highest malaria burden?

Potential Insight

Africa has the highest malaria case burden in the dashboard.

The difference between Africa and the other WHO regions is substantial, indicating a strong geographical concentration of malaria cases.

Question 4

Which WHO region has the highest estimated malaria mortality?

Potential Insight

Africa also has the highest estimated malaria deaths among the WHO regions displayed.

This demonstrates that the region carries both a disproportionately high number of malaria cases and malaria-related deaths.

Question 5

Which countries have the highest malaria case volumes?

Potential Insight

The Top 7 Countries visualization identifies Nigeria, Democratic Republic of the Congo, India, Mozambique, Uganda, Burkina Faso, and Ghana as the leading countries by malaria cases in the dashboard.

Nigeria has the largest displayed bar among these countries.

Question 6

Is a decline in malaria deaths accompanied by a similar decline in malaria cases?

Potential Insight

The two time-series charts show different patterns.

Estimated deaths generally decline across the period, while malaria cases decline initially and later increase.

This suggests that reductions in malaria mortality and malaria incidence do not necessarily move at exactly the same rate.

General Observations

The analysis demonstrates that malaria remains a major global public health challenge, with the burden concentrated primarily in Africa.

Although estimated malaria deaths show an overall decline from 2010 to 2017, malaria cases do not follow the exact same pattern. This highlights the importance of analyzing both incidence and mortality when evaluating malaria control programs.

The country-level analysis further demonstrates that malaria burden is concentrated in specific countries, creating opportunities for targeted interventions and resource allocation.

Analytical Considerations

The dashboard presents descriptive and comparative analysis.

The results should be interpreted carefully because:

Malaria case numbers are not necessarily directly comparable across countries without considering population size.
Reported cases can be influenced by differences in healthcare access and surveillance systems.
Estimated deaths are estimates rather than necessarily confirmed individual death records.
The dashboard covers only 2010–2017.
Observed trends do not automatically establish causation.
Country-level differences may reflect differences in population, reporting systems, climate, healthcare infrastructure, and malaria transmission intensity.
Limitations
1. Historical Dataset

The analysis covers 2010–2017 and therefore does not represent current malaria conditions.

2. Population Differences

Countries have different population sizes, so raw case counts should not be interpreted as risk rates.

3. Reporting Differences

Differences in surveillance and reporting systems can influence observed case numbers.

4. Estimated Mortality

The mortality figures displayed are estimated values and should be interpreted accordingly.

5. Descriptive Analysis

The dashboard identifies patterns but does not establish causal relationships.

Conclusion

The Global Malaria Burden: Cases, Mortality & Trends (2010–2017) project demonstrates how public health data can be transformed into an interactive analytical dashboard.

The analysis covers 107 countries and highlights significant geographical and temporal differences in malaria cases and estimated deaths. Africa emerges as the dominant malaria-burden region, while Nigeria leads the displayed Top 7 countries by malaria cases.

Although estimated malaria deaths show an overall downward trend, malaria cases fluctuate during the period, demonstrating the importance of continued surveillance, prevention, diagnosis, treatment, and targeted intervention.

The project demonstrates practical skills in data cleaning, data transformation, exploratory data analysis, dashboard development, data visualization, KPI creation, geographical analysis, and data storytelling.

The dashboard provides a foundation for deeper public health analysis and demonstrates how data-driven insights can support more targeted malaria-control strategies.
